Preoperative Facial Nerve Adhesion Probability Calculator

This research model applies only to vestibular schwannomas with a maximum diameter of 25 mm or less. Enter age, sex, maximum whole-tumor diameter on axial contrast-enhanced T1-weighted MRI (CPA + IAC), and preoperative PDW (%) to estimate the probability of intraoperative grade 3–4 facial nerve adhesion. The displayed lower, intermediate, and higher bands are descriptive internal reference bands, not externally validated clinical action thresholds. All calculations remain on this device; no patient information is uploaded or stored.

Surgical Classification of Facial Nerve Adhesion

Severe intraoperative adhesion is defined as grade 3–4 in this calculator.

Grade 1 · No adhesion
  • No resistance
  • Tumor and nerve anatomically separated
Grade 2 · Dissectible
  • Recognizable tissue plane exists
  • Freed with microdissectors alone
Grade 3 · Sharp dissection
  • Natural plane partially obliterated
  • Nerve stimulation often required
Grade 4 · Plane obliterated
  • No identifiable dissection interface
  • Sheet-like residual tumor on nerve

Illustrative classification cards; grades 3–4 correspond to the model endpoint.

Model Evidence and Intended Use

The primary outcome was grade 3–4 facial nerve adhesion recorded intraoperatively using a four-tier system before the PDW hypothesis was formulated. The development sample comprised 244 patients with tumors ≤25 mm and complete PDW data. The final linear equation underwent uniform bootstrap shrinkage and remains a provisional research specification.

244Complete-case development sample
176Severe adhesion events (72.1%)
0.799Conditional fixed PDW model AUC (95% CI 0.737–0.854)
0.776Selection-aware AUC (95% CI 0.708–0.836)

The clinical model (age, sex, and tumor size) had an AUC of 0.760. Adding PDW numerically changed fixed-model AUC by 0.039 (paired bootstrap 95% CI, −0.003 to 0.082) and Brier score by −0.0071 (95% CI, −0.0190 to 0.0054). Neither interval excluded no change. Selection-aware performance is the more conservative internal estimate.

View Variable Selection, Equation, and Limitations
  • Age and sex were prespecified demographic adjustment variables, and maximum tumor diameter was the prespecified imaging predictor. Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction was applied only to the 34 raw and derived blood markers; PDW was the only marker that remained significant after correction in both the full cohort and the ≤25-mm subgroup.
  • Koos grade was highly correlated with tumor size in the restricted development set and was therefore excluded from the main calculator to keep the equation continuous and transparent.
  • Provisional locked linear equation: logit(p)=0.7941889−0.0191572×Age+0.3169585×Male+0.2092349×Maximum tumor diameter−0.2121429×PDW.
  • The fixed PDW model had a Brier score of 0.160. When biomarker selection was repeated within each outer training fold, the Brier score was 0.166.
  • PDW was selected and modeled in the same dataset. The high outcome prevalence, single-center setting, single-surgeon assessment, and absence of external validation limit generalizability.
  • Post hoc specification checks did not show evidence of nonlinearity for tumor diameter or PDW within the ≤25-mm cohort. The linear equation remains provisional and requires external recalibration before prospective clinical use.
  • PDW refers to platelet distribution width reported as coefficient of variation (%). The local laboratory reference interval is 9–17%.
  • Internal reference bands shown on this page were derived from tertiles of locked-equation probabilities in the complete-case ≤25-mm development sample: lower <68%, intermediate 68–87%, and higher ≥87%. They are descriptive bands, not validated decision thresholds.
  • The model has not undergone external or adequately powered prospective validation and should not be used alone to alter resection extent, nerve management, or any other surgical decision.
